## 1. Abstract
A significant challenge in genomics is the interpretation of missense variants, with millions cataloged but only a small fraction clinically classified. This project addresses this gap by developing a sophisticated, gene-specific, multi-modal deep learning framework to predict the clinical significance of variants in the thiamine transporter genes **SLC19A2** and **SLC19A3**. By integrating three distinct data modalities—DNA sequence embeddings from **DNABERT-2**, protein structure embeddings from **ESM-2**, and engineered tabular features from population frequency and validation studies—the model achieves a comprehensive understanding of each variant. The resulting models demonstrate high accuracy, with test accuracies of **91.3%** for **SLC19A2** and **90.9%** for **SLC19A3**, significantly outperforming traditional prediction tools and successfully classifying thousands of previously un-annotated variants.

---

## 2. Motivation
- A vast number of human missense variants (over 4 million) are cataloged, yet only a small fraction (~2%) have been clinically classified.
- Specifically for genes like **SLC19A2** and **SLC19A3**, a significant number of known variants lack clinical annotation (e.g., for **SLC19A3**, ~490 labeled out of 16,000+).
- Traditional prediction tools (e.g., SIFT, PolyPhen-2) often underperform, particularly for rare variants, and may lack a deep contextual understanding of the genetic and protein sequences.

---

## 3. Research Objective
The primary objectives of this research are to:

1.  Develop customized prediction models tailored for specific genes of interest (**SLC19A2**, **SLC19A3**) to improve the predictability of variant effects.
2.  Leverage pretrained deep learning models (**DNABERT-2** for DNA and **ESM-2** for protein sequences) to generate rich, informative embeddings.
3.  Integrate multi-modal data (DNA embeddings, protein embeddings, and tabular features) to capture comprehensive information for each variant.
4.  Build a classifier to accurately categorize variants into five classes: Pathogenic, Likely Pathogenic, Benign, Likely Benign, and Normal.

---

## 4. Dataset and Preprocessing
-   **Data Source:** Initial variant data was sourced from NCBI's dbSNP database (`SLC19A2_dSNP.txt` & `SLC19A3_dSNP.txt`) and augmented with data from ClinVar.
-   **Gene Sequence Construction:** The complete reference sequence for each gene was extracted, including 2000 base pairs upstream and 1000 base pairs downstream to capture regulatory regions.
-   **Variant Simulation:** For each SNP, a "mutated" version of the full gene sequence was generated by incorporating the specific variation (e.g., A>C) at its precise location.
-   **Protein Sequence Generation:** Both the "mutated" and "normal" DNA sequences were transcribed and translated into their corresponding protein sequences.
-   **Labels:** Clinical significance labels were mapped to numerical values for model training: Pathogenic (-2), Likely Pathogenic (-1), Likely Benign (1), Benign (2), and Normal (3). Variants of "Uncertain Significance" (0) were excluded from the training set and used for post-training prediction.

## 5. Methodology
The model architecture integrates three data sources through distinct processing pipelines.
![ChatGPT Image Jun 10, 2025, 06_15_10 PM](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/07124a5f-5fbd-4f3f-8558-8653319e1f51)

### 5.1. DNA Embeddings (DNABERT-2)
The full DNA sequences (gene + flanking regions) containing each variant were input into a pretrained **DNABERT-2** model. To handle long sequences, each sequence was split into multiple parts, embedded individually, and the resulting vectors were concatenated to produce a final DNA embedding with a dimension of **3840**.

### 5.2. Protein Embeddings (ESM-2)
The translated protein sequences were processed by a pretrained **ESM-2** model (`esm2_t36_3B_UR50D`) to generate protein embeddings. This resulted in a vector representation for each protein with a dimension of **2560**.

### 5.3. Tabular Feature Engineering
A comprehensive set of features was engineered from variant frequency and validation data.

-   **Feature Creation:** For every frequency study found in the dataset (e.g., GnomAD, TOPMED, etc.), two columns were created: one binary `_is_mentioned` column (1 or 0) and one `_freq` column containing the variant's frequency (0 to 1).
-   **Aggregate Features:** Four summary features were created for each variant: `Min_Frequency`, `Max_Frequency`, `Avg_Frequency`, and `Total_Studies` mentioned.
-   **Validation Features:** Three binary features were created based on the `validation_status` column (`by-frequency`, `by-alfa`, `by-cluster`).
-   **Final Vector:** This process resulted in a tabular feature vector of **71** dimensions for **SLC19A2** and **73** dimensions for **SLC19A3**.

### 5.4. Fusion, Training, and Classification
The three sets of features were combined to train the final classifier.

-   **Scaling and Fusion:** The DNA embeddings (3840), protein embeddings (2560), and tabular features (~71) were independently scaled using a `StandardScaler` and then concatenated. This created a final fused, multi-modal embedding vector with a dimension of **~6471**.
-   **Classifier:** A deep neural network was trained on the fused embeddings to classify variants into one of the five clinical significance categories.

## 7. Conclusion
This work successfully demonstrates that a multi-modal deep learning architecture, tailored specifically to the **SLC19A2** and **SLC19A3** genes, can predict the clinical significance of DNA variants with high accuracy. By fusing genomic, proteomic, and population-level data, the model captures a holistic view of variant impact, far exceeding the performance of traditional methods. The high prediction accuracy and successful classification of thousands of previously uncertain variants provide a powerful tool for advancing pharmacogenomics and personalized medicine.
